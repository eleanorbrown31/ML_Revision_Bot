# EPA & Project Management

## EPA assessment plan

The EPA period only starts once the **employer** (not the training provider) confirms the apprentice is consistently working at or above the occupational standard. Apprentices need English and maths at Level 2 (Entry Level 3 minimum with an education/health/care plan) before starting. There are three assessment methods, all equally weighted: the **Project Report** (5,000 words +/-10%, based on a real work-based project, submitted 6 weeks after title confirmation) with **Presentation and supplementary questioning** (75 minutes, roughly 30 minutes presenting plus 45 minutes of at least 10 questions); the **Professional Discussion** (60 minutes, at least 10 open questions); and the **Technical Test** (4 long-response scenario questions, maximum 100 minutes, closed-book).

Each method grades Fail/Pass/Distinction; the overall grade is Fail (if any method fails, regardless of the others), Pass (all three pass), Merit (a pass in one plus distinction in two), or Distinction (all three distinction). A **re-sit** needs no further learning, a **re-take** does; only the failed method needs retaking, but a re-sat method caps the overall grade at Pass. Roles stay separate by design: the employer judges readiness and must not deliver the EPA; the training provider supports development but plays no part in the EPA itself; the EPAO delivers it independently.

## Project brief and pass criteria

The project brief, using the BCS Work-Based Project Sign-Off template, must be submitted to the EPAO at gateway **in the past tense** -- describing a project already completed, since Term 5 is when the actual project work happens (Term 4 is for planning). The brief's Project Checklist Table maps evidence against pass and distinction criteria across six themed areas: awareness of AI/data science business value (K13, K14), critically evaluating AI solution effectiveness (K23, S3, S17), applying systematic methodology and project management (S2, S9, S10, S22, S25), AI project and development management (K6, S24), communication and influencing skills (K28, S4, S5, S27, B2, B6), and application of technical knowledge (K1, K3, K5, K26, S11, S15, S18).

Across every theme, pass-level evidence describes what was done and why; distinction-level evidence goes further, evaluating trade-offs, adapting practice, and explaining impact on business outcomes -- description alone does not reach distinction. The three EPA methods each draw on a different cluster of KSBs: the Project Report on business value, statistics and communication; the Professional Discussion on problem-solving, strategy, industry trends and ethics; the Technical Test on data methods, systems integration and sources of error and bias.

## Project management methodologies

Several frameworks structure a data science project, each with a different shape. **CRISP-DM** (Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining) is cyclical: business and data understanding feed data preparation and modelling, then evaluation, then deployment, looping back to business understanding -- it can serve as the overarching framework for a project, or be nested inside a broader framework like SDLC when the AI component sits within a larger system. **TDSP** (Microsoft's Team Data Science Process) runs from business problem through data acquisition, modelling, and deployment/presentation to stakeholder acceptance. **KDD** (Knowledge Discovery in Datasets) moves from a data warehouse through selection, preprocessing and transformation to data mining, producing patterns that are evaluated into knowledge. **SEMMA** (SAS: Sample, Explore, Modify, Model, Assess) is covered in depth alongside Term 1's statistical investigations content.

General software methodologies -- Agile, Waterfall, Kanban, Scrum, SDLC -- can combine with these data-specific frameworks: Agile as the main framework with AI work as sprints, or the scientific method (hypothesis and test) used as a technique within a single stage rather than an overarching framework. The practical advice is to think through each project stage and pick whichever methods and tools genuinely fit it, rather than forcing one methodology across an entire project.
